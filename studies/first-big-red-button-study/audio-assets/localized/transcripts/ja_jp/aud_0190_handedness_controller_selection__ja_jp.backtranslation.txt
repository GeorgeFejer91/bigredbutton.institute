Handedness is measured because which hand handles a task can matter, especially with a controller and the Big Red Button. There are many other questionnaires that assess handedness more systematically. Some are not tiny. The Edinburgh Handedness Inventory is often used as a 10-item scale, the Annett Hand Preference Questionnaire uses 12 items, and the Waterloo Handedness Questionnaire has a 32-item version. They ask about concrete everyday acts, such as writing, throwing, and using scissors. Here, as questionnaire mercy, the study asks directly which left or right controller the participant will use for the experiment, because even that may subtly affect Big Red Button behavior.
